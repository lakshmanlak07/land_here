# Necessities: What You NEED to Settle In (Ireland)

These are not legal requirements, but you will want all of them sorted in your first week or two. They are the practical layer that makes daily life work.

---

## 1. An Irish Phone Plan / SIM Card

**What it is:**
An Irish mobile number and data plan on a local network.

**Why you need it:**
Your home SIM will roam expensively, and Irish services — bank logins, two-factor codes, GP and appointment bookings, rideshare, and university portals — assume a reachable Irish number. Many rental and job applications ask for one.

**What to look for:**
Prepaid (pay-as-you-go) SIMs are easy to set up without local credit history. The main networks are Vodafone, Three, and Eir; cheaper resellers (e.g. GoMo, 48, Tesco Mobile, Lyca) run on the same networks for less. Note that with Ireland in the EU, an Irish SIM gives you EU "roam like at home" data if you travel within the EU. Buy SIMs at supermarkets, phone shops, or convenience stores.

*Compare coverage for your specific city or town using each network's coverage map before you buy.*

---

## 2. A Leap Card (Public Transport)

**What it is:**
The **Leap Card** is Ireland's reloadable public transport smartcard, valid on bus, Luas (Dublin tram), DART, and commuter rail across much of the country.

**Why you need it:**
Leap fares are significantly cheaper than cash single fares, and the card caps your daily and weekly spend. Some services are card/contactless only.

**What to look for:**
There is a **Student Leap Card** that gives reduced fares — well worth getting if you're eligible (you typically verify your student status). There's also a Young Adult Leap Card for certain age ranges. Top up at machines, shops, or via the app.

*Verify the card types, student eligibility, and current fares on the official source: leapcard.ie and transportforireland.ie.*

## 3. Basic Furniture and Household Essentials

**What it is:**
The minimum to sleep, eat, and study: a bed and mattress, bedding, a pillow, towels, basic kitchenware, and a lamp.

**Why you need it:**
Purpose-built student accommodation is usually furnished, but private rentals and "digs" (renting a room in someone's home) vary. Ireland has a tight, expensive housing market, so sort your living situation early — necessities follow once you know what your place includes.

**What to look for:**
- **Adverts.ie and Facebook Marketplace** — the main second-hand markets; students moving out list furniture cheap or free.
- **DoneDeal** — popular Irish classifieds for larger items.
- **Charity shops** (e.g. St Vincent de Paul, Oxfam) — good for kitchenware and bedding.
- **IKEA, Dunnes Stores, Argos, and discount homeware shops** — affordable new basics.

Plan your first night: have bedding and a towel ready before you arrive.

*No official source — practical community knowledge. Your university's accommodation office often has arrival guidance and listings worth reading first.*

## 4. Groceries and Basic Food Setup

**What it is:**
Finding your nearest supermarket and setting up a basic pantry.

**Why you need it:**
Eating out in Ireland is expensive; cooking even simple meals saves a lot, which matters given high living costs.

**What to look for:**
- **Discounters: Aldi and Lidl** — cheapest for staples, widely available.
- **Full-range supermarkets: Tesco, Dunnes Stores, and SuperValu** — wider selection, slightly pricier.
- **Asian / international grocers** — Dublin, Cork, Galway, and Limerick have well-stocked shops with familiar ingredients, often cheaper for rice, spices, and lentils.
- Loyalty apps (Tesco Clubcard, SuperValu, Dunnes) and weekly specials cut costs.

Stock your first week with staples: bread, pasta/rice, oil, eggs, milk, and a few vegetables.

*Your university's international office often shares a local food and cost-of-living guide — check it on arrival.*

## 5. Weather-Appropriate Clothing

**What it is:**
Clothing for Ireland's mild but wet, windy, and changeable climate.

**Why you need it:**
Ireland rarely gets very hot or very cold, but it is famously **wet and unpredictable** — you can get sun, wind, and rain in a single afternoon, in any season. Winters are damp and grey with short daylight rather than deep cold.

**What to look for:**
- **A genuinely waterproof, windproof rain jacket** — your single most important item.
- **Waterproof shoes/boots** and layers you can add or remove as the weather shifts.
- A compact umbrella helps, though strong coastal wind often defeats them — a good hooded coat is more reliable.
- Charity shops are a good cheap source for coats and warm layers.

*Check Ireland's seasonal averages on the official source: Met Éireann (met.ie) before you pack or shop.*
