# Mandatories: What You MUST Do After Arriving in the UK

These are not optional. They are legal or institutional requirements for international students on a UK Student Visa (formerly Tier 4). Missing them can put your immigration status, your bank account, or your healthcare access at risk. Work through them in roughly this order.

---

## 1. Collect Your BRP / Confirm Your Digital Immigration Status

**What it is:**
Your UK Student Visa is often issued first as a **vignette sticker** in your passport that is valid for a short window (commonly 90 days) just to let you travel and enter the UK. It is not your full permission to stay. Depending on when and how your visa was issued, your full status is held either as a physical **Biometric Residence Permit (BRP)** you collect after arrival, or as a digital **eVisa** linked to a UKVI online account. The UK has been moving from physical BRPs to a fully digital eVisa system, so which one applies to you depends on your specific grant.

**Why it matters:**
This is the document (or digital record) that proves you are allowed to live, study, and work in the UK. You need it to open a bank account, register with a GP, prove your right to work, and re-enter the UK after travelling. Failing to collect a BRP by the stated deadline, or failing to set up your eVisa account, can cause serious problems.

**What you do:**
Read your visa decision letter carefully — it tells you whether you have a BRP to collect (and from which Post Office or your university) or a digital eVisa to access via a UKVI account. Do this within the first few days of arriving, and before any short deadline stated in your letter.

*Verify your exact process on the official source: gov.uk — search "BRP" or "eVisa / UKVI account." Confirm which system applies to your specific visa grant.*

---

## 2. Register / Enrol with Your University

**What it is:**
Formal enrolment (sometimes called registration or matriculation) with your university, plus a check-in with the international student support team who hold your Student Visa sponsorship.

**Why it matters:**
Your university is your **visa sponsor**. They are legally required to confirm to UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) that you have arrived and are studying. If you do not complete enrolment, your sponsorship can be withdrawn, which can end your visa. This is the UK equivalent of the importance of SEVIS check-in elsewhere.

**What you do:**
Complete online and in-person enrolment as instructed by your university, and attend any mandatory international student check-in. Bring your passport, your visa vignette or BRP, and proof of your UK address.

*Verify the exact enrolment steps and deadlines through your specific university's international student office. There is no single government page for this — it is set by your sponsor.*

---

## 3. Register with a GP (NHS Doctor)

**What it is:**
Registering with a local NHS **GP (General Practitioner) surgery** near where you live. You paid the **Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)** as part of your visa application, which gives you access to the NHS — but you still must actively register with a GP to use it for routine care.

**Why it matters:**
The GP is your gateway to most NHS healthcare — prescriptions, referrals to specialists, vaccinations, and non-emergency care. Registering before you are ill means you are not scrambling when you actually need help. Registration is free.

**What you do:**
Find a GP surgery whose catchment area covers your address, then complete their registration (often online or with a short form). You will usually be asked for proof of address and your immigration document. You do not need to be ill to register — do it as soon as you have an address.

*Verify the registration process on the official source: nhs.uk — "How to register with a GP surgery." Catchment and document requirements vary by surgery.*

---

## 4. Open a UK Bank Account

**What it is:**
A UK current account that lets you receive money, pay rent by bank transfer (very common in the UK), and avoid foreign transaction fees.

**Why it matters:**
Most UK rent, wages, and bills move by bank transfer or Direct Debit, which need a UK account. Relying on a foreign card means fees on every purchase and difficulty paying landlords. Some part-time employers can only pay into a UK account.

**What you do:**
High-street banks (Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander) typically ask for your passport, your BRP or visa, and proof of your UK address plus a university enrolment/bank letter. App-based "challenger" banks (Monzo, Starling, Revolut) can sometimes be opened more quickly and before your full documents are in order — useful as a stopgap.

*Verify current document requirements directly with each bank before you go in. The Money Helper service (moneyhelper.org.uk) has plain-English guidance on opening a UK bank account.*

---

## 5. Apply for a National Insurance (NI) Number — If You Will Work

**What it is:**
A **National Insurance (NI) number** is a personal reference number used for tax and social-security contributions when you work in the UK. A Student Visa usually permits limited part-time work (commonly up to 20 hours per week during term time — check your own visa conditions).

**Why it matters:**
If you take a part-time job, you need an NI number so your tax and National Insurance are recorded correctly. You can legally start work before the number arrives as long as you can prove your right to work, but you must apply for the number.

**What you do:**
Apply for an NI number online through the official government service once you are in the UK. You can generally only apply after you have arrived, and you may need to prove your identity and your right to work.

*Verify the current process and eligibility on the official source: gov.uk — "Apply for a National Insurance number." Confirm your specific visa's work conditions before taking any job.*

---

## 6. Police Registration — Only If Your Visa Requires It

**What it is:**
Historically, some students from certain countries were required to register with the police after arriving in the UK. **This requirement was abolished by the UK government in 2022.** It is included here only because older guidance still mentions it and may cause confusion.

**Why it matters:**
You should not be asked to do this anymore, but if your visa decision letter or older advice references police registration, you need to know whether it genuinely applies to you. In almost all current cases it does not.

**What you do:**
Check your visa decision letter. If it does not mention a police registration condition, you almost certainly do not need to do anything. If in doubt, confirm with your university's international student office rather than acting on outdated web pages.

*Verify the current position on the official source: gov.uk — confirm whether any police-registration condition applies to your specific grant. Treat older third-party pages with caution.*
