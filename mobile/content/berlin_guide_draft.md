## Banking

Berlin has a range of banking options well-suited to international students, including several that require minimal documentation to open.

The main German retail banks include Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, Sparkasse, and Volksbank. These offer full-service current accounts and are widely accepted for rent payments, direct debits, and salary. Opening an account typically requires your passport, proof of German residence (an Anmeldebescheinigung — the official address registration certificate obtained after registering at the Bürgeramt), and proof of student enrolment.

For students who have not yet completed their Anmeldung (address registration), several fintech alternatives are popular: N26, Revolut, and Wise all offer German or EU IBANs with minimal documentation and can be opened within a day or two of arrival. These are commonly used by students in the first weeks while administrative paperwork is being processed.

Germany runs on IBAN and BIC/SWIFT codes. You will need your IBAN for almost everything — paying rent, receiving money transfers, and setting up standing orders.

Cash is still commonly used in Germany, especially at smaller restaurants, markets, and local shops. Having some cash available is more important in Berlin than in many other major cities.

> Verify current details: documentation requirements for each bank change. Check the bank's website or visit a branch with your documents to confirm current account-opening requirements for international students.

## Healthcare

Germany has a high-quality universal healthcare system. As an international student enrolled at a German university, you are generally required to have health insurance, and most students qualify to join the statutory public health insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung, or GKV).

The major public health insurance funds (Krankenkassen) include TK (Techniker Krankenkasse), AOK, Barmer, and DAK. Students typically pay a relatively affordable monthly premium for comprehensive coverage. Enrolment in a Krankenkasse gives you access to GPs (Hausärzte), specialist referrals, hospital treatment, and prescribed medications.

To enrol, you need your passport, your student enrolment certificate (Immatrikulationsbescheinigung), and proof of German residence. Many Krankenkassen have English-speaking staff or online English resources.

Once enrolled, you will receive a health insurance card (Gesundheitskarte) which you present at every GP or specialist visit. Most consultations and treatments have no co-payment under standard GKV coverage.

Dental care is partially covered under GKV — routine check-ups and basic treatments are covered, while more complex procedures may require you to pay a portion of the cost.

> Verify current details: student health insurance contribution rates, eligibility rules, and the set of covered services are set by German law and can change. Check the websites of individual Krankenkassen and your university's international office for current enrolment procedures.

## SIM / Phone

Germany's major mobile carriers are Deutsche Telekom (T-Mobile), Vodafone, and O2. All operate in Berlin with good urban coverage. Discount sub-brands — including Aldi Talk, Congstar (Telekom network), and Blau (O2 network) — offer prepaid SIMs at significantly lower prices and are widely used by students.

Prepaid SIMs can be purchased at carrier stores, supermarkets (Aldi, Lidl, Rewe, Kaufland), electronics stores (Saturn, MediaMarkt), and convenience shops across Berlin. By law, all German SIM cards require identity verification — bring your passport. Activation is typically done in-store or via an ID verification video call.

Berlin's urban coverage is generally very good. Note that underground (U-Bahn) coverage has been improving but is not complete on all lines.

Monthly SIM-only plans without a contract are available and are the most flexible option for students. Many students opt for a plan with a German number and a large data bundle rather than relying solely on Wi-Fi.

Most Berlin university campuses offer free eduroam Wi-Fi, which is available to students at participating universities worldwide.

> Verify current details: SIM card plans, network coverage, and the ID verification process change. Check the carrier's website or visit a store to compare current options before buying.

## Transport

Berlin has one of the most comprehensive and affordable public transport networks in Europe, operated by BVG (Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe).

**The BVG network** includes the U-Bahn (underground), S-Bahn (urban rail, operated by Deutsche Bahn), trams (mainly in east Berlin), and buses. Together they cover the entire city and connect to the surrounding Brandenburg region.

**The Deutschlandticket** (previously called 9-Euro-Ticket) is a monthly national public transport pass valid on all regional and local public transport across Germany. At the time of writing, it offers extremely good value and is very popular with students. A discounted student version (Semesterticket) may be included in your university semester fee — check with your university's student services.

**Semesterticket** — most Berlin universities include a Semesterticket in the semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag) fee paid at the beginning of each semester. This typically covers unlimited BVG travel within Berlin's AB zone. Check your specific university's Semesterticket conditions when you enrol.

**Cycling** — Berlin is an extremely bike-friendly city with an extensive cycle path network. Buying a second-hand bicycle is very common among students and is often the cheapest and most convenient way to get around day-to-day. Bike theft is common; a good quality lock is essential.

**Walking** — central Berlin is walkable and the university districts (around Mitte, Prenzlauer Berg, Charlottenburg, and Dahlem) are well-connected to each other.

> Verify current details: the Deutschlandticket price, the Semesterticket inclusions, and BVG fare zones are subject to change. Check bvg.de and your university's student services website for current information.

## Cost of Living

Berlin has historically been one of the most affordable major European capitals for students, though costs have risen significantly over recent years — particularly for housing.

**Accommodation** — student dormitories (Studentenwohnheime) administered by Studentenwerk Berlin are the most affordable option but are limited in number and have waiting lists. Apply as early as possible, as applications often open many months in advance. Private shared flat rentals (Wohngemeinschaft, or WG) are the most common living arrangement for students in Berlin. Platforms like WG-Gesucht.de are the primary way to find WG rooms. Popular student neighbourhoods include Neukölln, Mitte, Prenzlauer Berg, Kreuzberg, and Friedrichshain.

**Food** — Berlin has many affordable dining options including döner kebabs (a Berlin institution), Turkish supermarkets, weekly street markets, and student cafeterias (Mensen) operated by Studentenwerk at very low prices. Supermarkets including Aldi, Lidl, Netto, and Penny offer competitive grocery prices.

**Transport** — if your Semesterticket covers your commute, transport costs can be very low.

**Culture and entertainment** — Berlin has one of the most active cultural scenes in Europe. Many museums and galleries offer significant student discounts or free admission. Concerts, club events, and street festivals are part of everyday Berlin life.

**Anmeldung (address registration)** — within a couple of weeks of arriving and moving into your accommodation, you are legally required to register your address at a local Bürgeramt. This is needed to open a bank account, get a SIM card, and for many other administrative processes. Book your Bürgeramt appointment online as slots can fill quickly.

> Verify current details: rental prices in Berlin have risen sharply and continue to change. Use current rental listings on WG-Gesucht, Immoscout24, or similar platforms, and check Studentenwerk Berlin's website for current dormitory application deadlines and costs.
