## Section: Mandatories

key: mandatories
title: Mandatories
tagline: Must-do legal and institutional steps tied to your Student visa (subclass 500). Skip these and your visa can be at risk — work through them roughly in order.
ordered: true

### Item
id: au-mand-enrol
title: Enrol and Confirm Your Course (CoE)
explanation: Your subclass 500 visa is granted on the condition that you study the registered course on your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE), and your provider reports your status to the government. Complete enrolment and make sure your CoE details match what you are actually studying.
source: { name: 'Department of Home Affairs (immi)', url: 'https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm exact steps with your university and your visa conditions on immi.

### Item
id: au-mand-oshc
title: Keep Your OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover) Active
explanation: Holding adequate health insurance (OSHC) for the whole of your stay is a condition of the subclass 500 visa, and international students generally cannot rely on Medicare. Confirm your policy is active and paid, and extend it if your course is extended.
source: { name: 'Australian Government Department of Health', url: 'https://www.health.gov.au' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm the OSHC requirement and approved providers; letting cover lapse is a visa breach.

### Item
id: au-mand-tfn
title: Get a Tax File Number (If You Will Work)
note: Only if you take paid work. The Student visa generally allows limited work — confirm current conditions.
explanation: A Tax File Number (TFN) keeps you from being taxed at the highest no-TFN rate and is used for superannuation. Apply free online through the Australian Taxation Office after you arrive — never pay a third party.
source: { name: 'Australian Taxation Office', url: 'https://www.ato.gov.au' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm work-hour limits before taking a job.

### Item
id: au-mand-bank
title: Open an Australian Bank Account
explanation: Rent, wages, and bills mostly move by bank transfer (and PayID/Osko), which need a local account. Major banks let you open a student/everyday account — bring your passport, visa grant, a local address, and your TFN once you have it.
source: { name: 'Moneysmart (Australian Government)', url: 'https://moneysmart.gov.au' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm documentation directly with each bank.

### Item
id: au-mand-address
title: Keep Your Provider Updated With Your Address
explanation: Student visa holders must give their education provider a current Australian residential address and update it promptly after moving. Your provider needs to be able to contact you, and keeping details current is part of staying compliant.
source: { name: 'Department of Home Affairs (immi)', url: 'https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm the exact reporting timeframe with your university and your visa conditions.

### Item
id: au-mand-conditions
title: Understand and Comply With Your Visa Conditions
explanation: Your visa grant notice lists conditions — maintaining enrolment and course progress, keeping OSHC, and staying within work-hour limits. Breaching them can lead to cancellation, so know your condition codes and ask before acting if unsure.
source: { name: 'Home Affairs — VEVO (check your conditions)', url: 'https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — read your own grant notice; conditions vary by individual.

## Section: Necessities

key: necessities
title: Necessities
tagline: Practical setup for daily life. Not legally required, but you'll want these sorted in your first week or two — in any order.
ordered: false

### Item
id: au-nec-sim
title: Get an Australian SIM / Phone Plan
explanation: An Australian number is assumed by two-factor authentication, MyGov and bank logins, rideshare, and rental and job forms. Prepaid SIMs are easy to set up without local credit history; resellers run on the big networks for less.
source: { name: '', url: '' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — compare each carrier’s coverage map for your city or regional campus before buying. No single official source.

### Item
id: au-nec-transit
title: Get a Public Transport Smartcard
explanation: Each state uses its own reloadable card or contactless tap-on (e.g. Opal in NSW, Myki in Victoria, Go card in Queensland), and these cap your daily and weekly spend. Whether international students get a concession (student) fare varies a lot by state.
source: { name: 'Your state transport authority', url: '' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — in some states full-fee international students are NOT eligible for concession travel; check before assuming.

### Item
id: au-nec-furniture
title: Sort Basic Furniture and Household Essentials
explanation: Student accommodation ranges from fully furnished to empty, and most private share-house rooms are unfurnished. Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree, student groups, and op shops (charity stores) are the cheapest sources.

id: au-nec-groceries
title: Find Groceries
explanation: Eating out in Australia adds up fast, so cooking simple meals saves a lot over a semester. Coles and Woolworths are everywhere, Aldi is cheaper for staples, and most cities have international grocers with familiar ingredients.

### Item
id: au-nec-clothing
title: Get Weather-Appropriate Clothing
explanation: Australia’s seasons are reversed from the Northern Hemisphere and climates vary enormously — tropical in the north, genuinely cold winters in the south. Sun protection (hat, sunglasses, high-SPF sunscreen) matters year-round because UV is strong even on cool days.
