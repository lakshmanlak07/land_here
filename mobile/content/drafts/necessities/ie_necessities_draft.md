## Section: Mandatories

key: mandatories
title: Mandatories
tagline: Must-do legal and institutional steps (typically Stamp 2 student permission). Skip these and your status or ability to work can be at risk — work through them roughly in order.
ordered: true

### Item
id: ie-mand-enrol
title: Register Your Course Enrolment
explanation: Your permission to stay is conditional on full-time enrolment in an eligible course (generally on the Interim List of Eligible Programmes, ILEP). Complete registration and get an enrolment letter — you will need it for immigration and to open a bank account.
source: { name: 'Irish Immigration Service', url: 'https://www.irishimmigration.ie' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm eligible-course rules and your university’s exact steps.

### Item
id: ie-mand-irp
title: Register With Immigration and Get Your IRP Card
explanation: Non-EU/EEA students staying longer than 90 days must register their permission and receive an Irish Residence Permit (IRP) card — your official proof of legal status. Slots can be limited and there are deadlines tied to your entry permission, so book as early as possible.
source: { name: 'Irish Immigration Service', url: 'https://www.irishimmigration.ie' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — process, fees, documents, and locations differ inside vs outside Dublin.

### Item
id: ie-mand-pps
title: Get a PPS Number (If You Will Work or Need Public Services)
note: A Stamp 2 permission generally allows limited part-time work — confirm current hour limits before taking a job.
explanation: A Personal Public Service (PPS) Number is your reference number for tax and public services; without one you can be emergency-taxed at a high rate. Apply through the official MyWelfare process once you have an address and a reason such as a job offer.
source: { name: 'Gov.ie / MyWelfare', url: 'https://www.gov.ie' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm the current application process and work-hour limits.

### Item
id: ie-mand-bank
title: Open an Irish Bank Account
explanation: An Irish current account with a SEPA IBAN is needed for wages, rent, and bills and avoids foreign-card fees. Main banks usually ask for your passport, IRP/immigration registration, proof of an Irish address, and an enrolment letter; app-based banks are widely used as a faster stopgap.
source: { name: 'Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC)', url: 'https://www.ccpc.ie' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm documentation directly with each bank.

### Item
id: ie-mand-insurance
title: Hold Valid Private Medical Insurance
explanation: Non-EU/EEA students are generally required to have private medical insurance, and you must show proof when you register with immigration. International students typically do not have automatic access to free public healthcare, so make sure cover spans your whole stay.
source: { name: 'Irish Immigration Service', url: 'https://www.irishimmigration.ie' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm the exact requirement and accepted cover; your university may offer an approved plan.

## Section: Necessities

key: necessities
title: Necessities
tagline: Practical setup for daily life. Not legally required, but you'll want these sorted in your first week or two — in any order.
ordered: false

### Item
id: ie-nec-sim
title: Get an Irish SIM / Phone Plan
explanation: An Irish number is assumed by bank logins, two-factor codes, GP and appointment bookings, and university portals. Prepaid SIMs are easy to set up without local credit history, and an Irish (EU) SIM also gives you “roam like at home” data elsewhere in the EU.
source: { name: '', url: '' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — compare each network’s coverage map for your city or town. No single official source.

### Item
id: ie-nec-leap
title: Get a Leap Card (Public Transport)
explanation: The Leap Card is Ireland’s reloadable transport smartcard, valid on bus, Luas, DART, and commuter rail, and is much cheaper than cash single fares with daily/weekly caps. A Student Leap Card gives reduced fares if you are eligible.
source: { name: 'Leap Card / Transport for Ireland', url: 'https://www.leapcard.ie' }
is_verified: false
flags: NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm card types, student eligibility, and current fares.

### Item
id: ie-nec-furniture
title: Sort Basic Furniture and Household Essentials
explanation: Purpose-built student accommodation is usually furnished, but private rentals and “digs” vary, and Ireland’s housing market is tight and expensive — sort your living situation early. Adverts.ie, DoneDeal, Facebook Marketplace, and charity shops are the cheapest sources.

### Item
id: ie-nec-groceries
title: Find Groceries
explanation: Eating out in Ireland is expensive, so cooking simple meals saves a lot given high living costs. Discounters (Aldi, Lidl) are cheapest for staples; Tesco, Dunnes, and SuperValu have wider selection; loyalty apps and weekly specials cut costs.

### Item
id: ie-nec-clothing
title: Get Weather-Appropriate Clothing
explanation: Ireland is rarely very hot or very cold but is famously wet, windy, and changeable in any season, with damp grey winters. A genuinely waterproof, windproof rain jacket plus waterproof shoes and layers is your most important clothing.
