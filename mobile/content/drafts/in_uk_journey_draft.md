## Phase 1: After Acceptance

### Step 1 — Receive your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)

Once you have formally accepted your offer, your university will assign you a **Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)** number. The CAS is a unique reference number that your university holds in the UK Home Office's online system. You do not receive a physical document — you receive the number, along with a CAS statement that lists the details of your offer.

You cannot apply for your UK Student Visa without a CAS. Your CAS statement will include your course start date, course end date, the course level, the tuition fee you have been assessed, and whether you have met the financial requirements.

**Why it matters:** The visa application form will ask for your CAS number. The Home Office will cross-reference the details you provide in your application against the CAS record held by your university. Any discrepancy can cause delays or refusal.

> Official source: UK Visas and Immigration — CAS guidance for students
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/apply

---

### Step 2 — Review your CAS statement for accuracy

Before you begin the visa application, carefully read your CAS statement. Verify that your full name matches your passport exactly, your date of birth is correct, and the course details match your offer letter. If anything is wrong, contact your university's international admissions office immediately to have the CAS corrected.

> Official source: UK Visas and Immigration — Student Visa
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa

## Phase 2: Visa Process

### Step 1 — Check whether you need a Student Visa

Most Indian nationals studying in the UK for more than six months require a **Student Visa** (formerly called the Tier 4 visa). Short courses of six months or less may use a Short-term Study Visa instead. Check the current eligibility rules on the official UK government website.

> Official source: UK Visas and Immigration — Check if you need a UK visa
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/check-uk-visa

---

### Step 2 — Pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)

Before submitting your visa application, you must pay the **Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)**. This is a per-year fee that gives you access to NHS healthcare during your stay. The amount is set annually by the UK government and must be paid in full upfront for the duration of your visa. Keep your IHS payment reference — you will need it to complete your visa application.

> Official source: UK government — Pay the Immigration Health Surcharge
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application

---

### Step 3 — Complete the online Student Visa application (form VAF1A / online form)

Apply for the Student Visa online through the official UK Visas and Immigration portal. You will need to provide your CAS number, passport details, financial evidence, English language qualification, ATAS certificate if required for your subject, and your IHS payment reference.

**Financial evidence:** You must show that you have enough money to pay your tuition fees (if not already paid) and to support yourself during your studies. The required amount per month and the time period for which funds must be held are set by the Home Office — check the current official guidance.

**English language requirement:** Most Indian nationals satisfy the English language requirement if their degree is taught in English. Check the current exemptions and accepted qualifications.

> Official source: UK Visas and Immigration — Student Visa application
> URL: https://apply-to-visit-or-stay-in-the-uk.homeoffice.gov.uk/

---

### Step 4 — Book and attend your biometrics appointment

After submitting your application online, you will be directed to book an appointment at a UKVCAS (UK Visa and Citizenship Application Services) service point in India to enrol your biometric information (fingerprints and photograph). Major service points are located in cities including Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, and Bangalore. Bring your passport and your appointment confirmation.

> Official source: UKVCAS — Biometrics appointment booking
> URL: https://www.ukvcas.co.uk/

---

### Step 5 — Submit supporting documents via the online portal

After your biometrics appointment, you may be required to upload supporting documents via the online UKVCAS portal. Documents typically include your CAS statement, financial evidence (bank statements), academic qualifications, and English language certificates. Follow the document checklist generated during your application.

> Official source: UK Visas and Immigration — Student Visa supporting documents
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/documents-you-must-provide

## Phase 3: Before Departure

### Step 1 — Arrange accommodation before you arrive

Secure accommodation before leaving India. Most UK universities guarantee a place in university-managed halls of residence for first-year international students if you apply by the stated deadline. Apply as soon as you accept your offer. If you are in the private rental market, sign a tenancy agreement with a confirmed start date before you travel — arriving without a confirmed address is stressful and can complicate your BRP collection.

> Official source: Study in the UK — general guidance
> URL: https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/Information--Advice/Studying--living-in-the-UK/Accommodation

---

### Step 2 — Collect your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) after arrival

Your Student Visa may come in the form of a **vignette sticker** in your passport valid for 30 days. This is not your full leave to remain — it is only a travel document to enter the UK. Once in the UK you must collect your **Biometric Residence Permit (BRP)** from the Post Office branch specified in your visa decision letter, usually within 10 days of arrival or before the vignette expires.

Your BRP is your main identity document in the UK and you will need it to open a bank account, register with a GP, and for various university processes.

> Note: The BRP system may be replaced or modified by the UK government — verify the current entry clearance process on gov.uk before travelling.
> Official source: UK government — Biometric Residence Permits
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/biometric-residence-permits

---

### Step 3 — Book your flight within the entry window

Your visa vignette specifies the earliest date you may enter the UK. Do not book a flight to arrive before this date. Also note the relationship between your arrival date, your course start date, and the 10-day BRP collection deadline.

> Official source: UK Visas and Immigration — Entering the UK
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/when-you-arrive

---

### Step 4 — Organise your travel documents

Carry all critical documents in your hand luggage: your passport with UK visa vignette, your CAS statement or CAS number noted separately, your university offer/acceptance letter, financial evidence, your IHS payment reference, and your BRP collection letter. UK Border Force officers may ask to see supporting documents at the port of entry.

> Official source: UK government — What to bring to the UK
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/when-you-arrive

## Phase 4: After Arrival

### Step 1 — Collect your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP)

Collect your BRP from the Post Office branch listed in your visa decision letter. Do this promptly — missing the BRP collection deadline can affect your immigration status.

> Official source: UK government — Biometric Residence Permits
> URL: https://www.gov.uk/biometric-residence-permits/arrived

---

### Step 2 — Register with your university's international student office

Within the first few days, complete your university enrolment and register with the international student office. This activates your student record, which is required for the university to confirm your attendance in the UKVI system. Bring your passport, BRP, and any documents your university requests.

> Official source: UKCISA — Arriving in the UK
> URL: https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/Information--Advice/Studying--living-in-the-UK/Arriving-in-the-UK

---

### Step 3 — Register with a GP

Register with a local NHS GP as soon as you have a UK address. Your university health centre or local GP surgery will guide you through the registration process. You will need your BRP and proof of your UK address.

> Official source: NHS — How to register with a GP
> URL: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/how-to-register-with-a-gp-surgery/

---

### Step 4 — Open a UK bank account

A UK bank account is required for receiving your stipend or part-time wages, paying rent by bank transfer, and managing everyday expenses. Most UK banks require your BRP, proof of address, and your university enrolment letter. Some challeger banks (Monzo, Revolut) can be opened before you have your BRP.

> Official source: Money Helper — Opening a bank account
> URL: https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/everyday-money/banking/how-to-open-a-bank-account

---

### Step 5 — Get a UK SIM card

A local UK phone number is needed for bank account verification, university systems, and daily communication. Prepaid SIMs from EE, O2, Vodafone, Three, or discount sub-brands can be bought at most supermarkets and phone shops without any documentation beyond your identity.

> Official source: Ofcom — Choosing a mobile provider
> URL: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/making-calls/choosing-a-provider
