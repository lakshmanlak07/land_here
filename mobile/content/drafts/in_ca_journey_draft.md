## Phase 1: After Acceptance

### Step 1 — Receive your Letter of Acceptance (LOA)

Once you have accepted your offer, your designated learning institution (DLI) will issue you a **Letter of Acceptance (LOA)**. This is the official document confirming your enrolment and is required for your Study Permit application. The LOA will state the course name, duration, tuition fee, and the school's DLI number (a government-issued designation confirming the school is approved to host international students).

**Important:** Only students enrolled at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) are eligible to apply for a Canadian Study Permit. Confirm your school's DLI status at the IRCC website.

> Official source: IRCC — Designated Learning Institutions list
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare/designated-learning-institutions-list.html

---

### Step 2 — Review your Letter of Acceptance for accuracy

Check that your name matches your passport exactly, the course dates and tuition figures are correct, and the DLI number is present. Errors in the LOA can cause Study Permit delays. Contact your university's international admissions team immediately if anything needs correcting.

> Official source: IRCC — How to apply for a Study Permit
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/apply.html

## Phase 2: Visa Process

### Step 1 — Check whether you need a Study Permit and a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV)

Most Indian nationals studying in Canada for more than six months require both a **Study Permit** and a **Temporary Resident Visa (TRV)** (also called a Canadian visitor visa). In most cases, IRCC processes both together when you apply for a Study Permit, so you do not need to apply for the TRV separately. Confirm the current requirements for Indian citizens on the IRCC website.

> Official source: IRCC — Find out if you need a study permit
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit.html

---

### Step 2 — Gather your documents before applying

Before submitting your Study Permit application, gather the following documents. Requirements may vary; confirm the current list on the IRCC website:

- Valid passport (with at least 6 months validity beyond your intended stay)
- Letter of Acceptance from your DLI
- Proof of financial support (bank statements, scholarship letters, or a combination) covering tuition and living costs for the duration of your studies
- Statement of Purpose or Study Plan explaining why you chose Canada and this specific program
- Academic transcripts and certificates (all prior education)
- Language test results (IELTS, TOEFL, or equivalent — check if your program requires one)
- Two recent passport-size photographs
- Proof of ties to India (to demonstrate intent to return after studies)

> Official source: IRCC — Study Permit: Who can apply
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/apply.html

---

### Step 3 — Submit your Study Permit application online via IRCC

Apply through the official IRCC online application portal. Create a secure account on the IRCC website, complete the application form, upload your supporting documents, and pay the Study Permit application fee (non-refundable). If a Temporary Resident Visa is also required, this is processed as part of the same application at no additional visa fee.

Note your application number — you will use it to check your application status online.

> Official source: IRCC — Apply online for a Study Permit
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/apply.html

---

### Step 4 — Provide biometrics (fingerprints and photograph)

If it is your first Study Permit application, or if a certain period has passed since your last Canadian biometrics enrolment, you will be asked to provide biometrics. After submitting your application and paying the biometrics fee, you will receive a Biometric Instruction Letter (BIL). Use this letter to book an appointment at a **Visa Application Centre (VAC)** operated by VFS Global in India. VAC locations include Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, and others.

> Official source: IRCC — Provide biometrics
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/biometric/apply-in-person.html
> VFS Global India — VAC locations
> URL: https://www.vfsglobal.ca/canada/india/

---

### Step 5 — Track your application and wait for a decision

Processing times vary significantly. Check the current estimated processing times on the IRCC website using the Application Processing Times tool — do not rely on any unofficial estimate. Processing times for Indian applicants have varied widely in recent years and are updated regularly.

If IRCC needs additional documents (a procedural fairness letter) you will be notified by email at the address you provided in your application. Monitor your IRCC account and your email regularly.

> Official source: IRCC — Check processing times
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/check-processing-times.html

---

### Step 6 — Receive your Port of Entry (POE) Letter of Introduction

If your Study Permit application is approved, IRCC will issue you a **Port of Entry (POE) Letter of Introduction** and, if required, stamp a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV) into your passport. The POE Letter of Introduction is NOT the actual Study Permit — it is a document authorising you to travel to Canada and collect your Study Permit from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) officer at the port of entry.

Print the POE Letter and carry it in your hand luggage along with all other supporting documents.

> Official source: IRCC — What happens after you apply for a Study Permit
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/after-applying.html

## Phase 3: Before Departure

### Step 1 — Arrange accommodation before you leave India

Secure your accommodation before arriving in Canada. If your university provides guaranteed residence for first-year international students, apply through your university's housing portal immediately after accepting your offer. If you are renting privately, confirm your lease start date and address before you travel — you will need a Canadian address for your SIN application and banking.

> Official source: IRCC — Prepare to arrive in Canada
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare-arrival.html

---

### Step 2 — Arrange health insurance for the gap period

Depending on your Canadian province and your university's health plan, there may be a waiting period before provincial health coverage begins. Many universities automatically enrol international students in a supplementary health and dental plan. Review your university's health insurance information before you depart so you know what you are covered for from day one of arrival.

> Official source: Your university's international student health coverage page (URL will vary by institution)

---

### Step 3 — Book your flight within the authorised entry period

Your TRV has a validity period — you must enter Canada before it expires. Also note the latest start date in your POE Letter. Plan your arrival so you have enough time before your course begins to handle administrative tasks (SIN application, bank account, campus check-in).

> Official source: IRCC — Entering Canada with a Study Permit
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare-arrival.html

---

### Step 4 — Organise documents to carry in your hand luggage

Carry in your hand luggage (never in checked baggage): your passport with TRV, POE Letter of Introduction, Letter of Acceptance, proof of financial support (bank statements), housing confirmation, and any other documents listed on the IRCC website. A CBSA officer at the port of entry will issue your Study Permit on the spot and may ask to see supporting documents.

> Official source: IRCC — When you arrive at a port of entry
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare-arrival.html

## Phase 4: After Arrival

### Step 1 — Receive your Study Permit at the port of entry

At Canadian customs and immigration, present your passport, POE Letter of Introduction, and supporting documents to the CBSA officer. They will assess your admissibility and, if approved, issue your **Study Permit** as a physical document. Read it carefully before leaving the immigration hall — check your name, the expiry date, and any conditions on it. Report any errors to the CBSA officer immediately.

> Official source: IRCC — What happens at the port of entry
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare-arrival.html

---

### Step 2 — Apply for a Social Insurance Number (SIN)

A **Social Insurance Number (SIN)** is a 9-digit number required to work in Canada, file a tax return, and access certain government services. If your Study Permit authorises you to work (most do, up to a set number of hours per week during academic sessions), apply for your SIN at a Service Canada Centre as soon as possible after arrival. Bring your Study Permit and passport.

> Official source: Service Canada — Apply for a Social Insurance Number
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/apply.html

---

### Step 3 — Register with your university's international student office

Complete your university enrolment and register with the international student office within the first few days of your arrival. This activates your student record and is required to maintain your Study Permit status. Bring your Study Permit, passport, and any documents your university requests.

> Official source: Your university's international student services page (URL will vary by institution)

---

### Step 4 — Open a Canadian bank account

A Canadian bank account is needed for receiving your scholarship or employment income, paying rent, and everyday expenses. The five major Canadian banks (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC) all offer student chequing accounts with no monthly fee. You will typically need your Study Permit, passport, and a Canadian address. Some banks offer pre-arrival account opening — check your chosen bank's website.

> Official source: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada — Opening a bank account
> URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/opening-bank-account.html

---

### Step 5 — Get a Canadian SIM card

A local Canadian phone number is needed for banking, university systems, and daily life. Prepaid SIMs from Rogers, Bell, Telus, or their sub-brands (Fido, Virgin Plus, Koodo) are available at carrier stores, supermarkets, and electronics retailers without a credit check or SIN.

> Official source: CRTC — Your rights as a wireless customer
> URL: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/mobile/rights.htm
