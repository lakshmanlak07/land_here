## Banking

Vancouver has branches of all five major Canadian banks — RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, and CIBC — across the city and near the two major university campuses: UBC (University of British Columbia) in Point Grey and SFU (Simon Fraser University) in Burnaby.

Most major banks offer student chequing accounts with no monthly fee for full-time enrolled students. To open one you typically need your passport, your study permit or a copy of your acceptance letter (if the permit hasn't arrived yet), and a Canadian address. Some banks offer a pre-arrival banking programme for international students arriving at select Canadian universities — check your bank's website for details before you leave home.

If you need an account quickly on arrival, fintech apps like Tangerine, Neo Financial, Koho, and Wise offer Canadian account numbers and debit cards with no monthly fees and minimal documentation requirements. These are useful in the first week before you visit a branch.

Bank of Montreal (BMO) and TD have branches directly on the UBC campus. RBC and Scotiabank have branches near Commercial Drive and in downtown Vancouver.

> Verify current details: student account conditions, pre-arrival banking availability, and fee structures change. Check each bank's website or visit your university's international student centre before opening an account.

## Healthcare

Healthcare coverage for international students in British Columbia (BC) depends on your visa duration and your university's plan.

Most students on a study permit of more than six months who have been in BC for three months become eligible to apply for BC's Medical Services Plan (MSP). However, during the three-month waiting period you are not covered by MSP and need private health insurance.

Most universities in Vancouver — including UBC and SFU — automatically enrol international students in a university health and dental plan that bridges the MSP waiting period and provides supplementary coverage (dental, prescription drugs, vision) even after MSP kicks in. Review what your university's plan covers before you arrive.

After completing the MSP waiting period, you will need to enrol in MSP yourself. UBC's international student office has guides for this process.

For GP (family doctor) services, both UBC and SFU have on-campus student health centres. Walk-in clinics are also available across Vancouver for non-emergency medical needs.

> Verify current details: MSP eligibility rules, the three-month waiting period, and university health plan coverage are all subject to change. Check the BC government's MSP information page and your university's student services site for the most current guidance.

## SIM / Phone

Vancouver has excellent mobile coverage from Canada's three major carriers: Rogers, Bell, and Telus. Their respective flanker brands — Fido (Rogers), Virgin Plus (Bell), and Koodo (Telus) — typically offer the same network quality at lower prices and are popular with students.

Prepaid SIM cards can be purchased at carrier stores throughout downtown, in malls (Pacific Centre, Metropolis at Metrotown), at Walmart, Best Buy, Shoppers Drug Mart, and at Vancouver International Airport. No credit check or SIN is required for prepaid.

For students at UBC, the Point Grey campus is well-covered by all three networks. Bring an unlocked phone and passport for in-store activation.

Monthly plans in Canada have historically been more expensive than in many other countries, but prices for prepaid and student-targeted plans have come down with increased competition. Check carrier websites for current student promotions, which are often launched at the start of the academic year in September.

Campus buildings at UBC and SFU both offer free eduroam Wi-Fi to students.

> Verify current details: plan pricing and coverage change frequently. Compare current offers on carrier websites or a price-comparison platform before committing.

## Transport

Vancouver's public transit is operated by TransLink, which covers the Metro Vancouver region with SkyTrain (rapid transit), buses, the SeaBus ferry, and the West Coast Express commuter rail.

**Compass Card** is TransLink's reusable transit card. You tap in and tap out on SkyTrain and SeaBus; buses are tap-in only. Compass Cards can be purchased at SkyTrain stations and loaded with money or monthly passes at vending machines, online, or at London Drugs stores. Contactless bank card payment is also accepted on most TransLink services.

**SkyTrain** has three lines (Expo, Millennium, Canada) and reaches UBC bus exchange, downtown, Surrey, Richmond, and Burnaby (SFU's Burnaby campus is served by the Millennium Line). Journey times from downtown to UBC take around 45–50 minutes by transit (bus connections required from Broadway–City Hall SkyTrain station).

**Buses** are extensive, particularly the R4 and 99 B-Line rapid bus routes along Broadway that connect downtown to UBC. These run frequently, including late evenings.

**UPass BC** — students at UBC and SFU pay a student transit pass fee as part of their tuition. This gives unlimited TransLink travel within the Metro Vancouver fare zone for the academic year. It is significantly cheaper than buying monthly passes individually. Check with your university's student services to confirm you are enrolled.

**Cycling** — Vancouver has an extensive network of separated cycle lanes and the Mobi Bike Share scheme operates across the city. UBC has excellent cycling infrastructure and a dedicated campus route.

**SeaBus** — the passenger ferry across Burrard Inlet to North Vancouver is included in the regular TransLink fare and is a scenic option.

> Verify current details: TransLink fares, UPass details, and service changes are updated regularly. Check translink.ca for current fares and route maps.

## Cost of Living

Vancouver is one of Canada's most expensive cities, particularly for housing, which is a significant consideration when budgeting.

**Accommodation** — on-campus residence at UBC is the most convenient option for first-year students. UBC guarantees housing to first-year domestic students and prioritises international students, but applications must be submitted promptly after accepting your offer. Off-campus rentals in Vancouver are expensive by Canadian standards. More affordable areas include East Vancouver (Commercial Drive area), Burnaby, and New Westminster, all accessible by SkyTrain or bus.

**Food** — Vancouver has exceptional food diversity, reflecting its large East and Southeast Asian communities. Affordable options include the many food courts in Richmond, the restaurants along Main Street and Commercial Drive, and Asian grocery stores such as T&T Supermarket and H Mart, which offer competitive prices on rice, noodles, vegetables, and fresh produce. Cooking at home from grocery stores (including No Frills, Superstore, and Save-On-Foods) is considerably cheaper than dining out regularly.

**Transport** — the UPass (included in student fees) significantly reduces transport costs if you use TransLink regularly. If you live within cycling or walking distance of campus you can keep transport costs very low.

**Outdoor life** — one of Vancouver's great advantages for students is access to free or low-cost outdoor activities: hiking the North Shore mountains (Grouse, Seymour, Cypress), kayaking in Indian Arm, skiing in winter (via the SkyBus to Cypress), and visiting Pacific Spirit Regional Park adjacent to UBC — all at minimal cost.

**Taxes** — prices in British Columbia include GST (5%) and in some cases PST (7%). Restaurant meals are taxed; basic groceries generally are not. This affects your effective cost of food and goods.

> Verify current details: Vancouver rental prices are among the most variable in Canada and change with the housing market. Use current rental listings, your university's cost-of-living estimates, and talk to current students before finalising your budget.
