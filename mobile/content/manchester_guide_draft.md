## Banking

Manchester is one of the UK's major financial and commercial cities and has a strong presence of all the main high-street banks. Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander, and Halifax all have multiple branches in the city centre and near the university districts of Oxford Road, Fallowfield, and Withington.

Most banks offer student accounts with no monthly fee for full-time students. These typically include a free railcard, interest-free overdraft, or cashback offer. Opening one requires your passport or biometric residence permit (BRP), proof of your UK university enrolment, and a UK address. Many banks near the University of Manchester and Manchester Metropolitan University (MMU) are experienced with international student applications.

If you arrive before your high-street account is ready, fintech apps like Monzo, Revolut, and Wise can be set up on your phone the same day, give you a sort code and account number, and are accepted for most bank transfers including rent payments.

Cash machines (ATMs) are widely available across Manchester, including at Piccadilly and Victoria train stations, the Arndale shopping centre, and across the student areas.

> Verify current details: student account offers, eligibility conditions, and overdraft limits change each academic year. Visit the bank in person or check their website for current terms before applying.

## Healthcare

As an international student on a UK student visa of more than six months, you will have paid the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) as part of your visa application. This gives you access to NHS (National Health Service) healthcare during your studies.

Your first step is to register with a GP (General Practitioner — a local doctor). The University of Manchester has an on-campus health service, and both UoM and MMU maintain lists of local GP practices where students can register. Many practices near the university areas in Fallowfield, Rusholme, and the Oxford Road corridor welcome student registrations.

NHS GP appointments are free at the point of use once you are registered. For urgent medical advice, NHS 111 is free to call (or use online) at any time. For emergencies, Manchester Royal Infirmary and Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust hospitals provide 24/7 A&E services.

Manchester also has excellent mental health and wellbeing support through the universities' own services. If you are struggling, the university counselling and wellbeing teams are a good first contact.

> Verify current details: NHS procedures, IHS coverage terms, and GP registration processes are set by the UK government and NHS. Check NHS.uk and your university's health pages for the most up-to-date guidance before you travel.

## SIM / Phone

Getting a UK SIM card in Manchester is easy. The main high street — Market Street and the Arndale area — has stores for EE, O2, Vodafone, and Three. Supermarkets including Tesco, Sainsbury's, and Asda also stock SIM cards at low cost.

No credit check or proof of address is needed for a prepaid SIM. Bring your unlocked phone and you can be set up within minutes. Major carriers all have solid coverage in central Manchester and across the student areas (Fallowfield, Withington, Didsbury, and the Northern Quarter). If you plan to travel across the UK, EE is generally considered to have the widest national coverage.

Monthly rolling SIM-only plans (no contract) start at low price points and typically include unlimited calls, texts, and a data allowance. Switching to a postpaid plan later is easy once you have a UK bank account and some credit history.

The universities' campus buildings also have eduroam Wi-Fi, which is available to students from partner institutions worldwide.

> Verify current details: plan pricing and network coverage change frequently. Compare plans on carrier websites or a comparison site. Student deals often appear at the start of term in September.

## Transport

Manchester has one of the most extensive public transport networks outside London, operated by Transport for Greater Manchester (TfGM).

**Metrolink tram** is the backbone of Manchester's surface transport. The network covers the city centre, MediaCityUK (Salford Quays), Trafford, the airport, and many suburbs. Tram stops are centrally located at Piccadilly Gardens, Deansgate, and St Peter's Square, all within easy reach of universities.

**Buses** are extensive and serve areas that trams do not cover, including Fallowfield and Withington (where many student houses are). Several key routes run frequently and late into the evening.

**Contactless payment and the Get Me There app** — most Manchester trams and buses accept contactless bank card payment. The Get Me There app lets you buy tram tickets and top up a smart card. Buses increasingly accept contactless too.

**Student travel discounts** — Greater Manchester offers 16–25 discount passes on some services. Check TfGM's website for the current options available to students.

**Cycling** — Bee Network cycling infrastructure has expanded significantly in Manchester and Salford, with protected lanes on many key routes. Beryl Bikes (formerly Mobike) operates a bike-share scheme in parts of the city.

**Walking** — the university campus and the city centre are very walkable. Many student houses in Fallowfield are less than 30 minutes on foot from the Oxford Road campus.

> Verify current details: tram and bus routes, fares, and the student discount options change. Check tfgm.com for current network maps and prices.

## Cost of Living

Manchester is significantly more affordable than London, which makes it one of the UK's most popular student cities for value alongside academic quality.

**Accommodation** — university halls are available through both UoM and MMU and are the easiest option for first-year international students. Private shared houses in Fallowfield, Withington, Rusholme, and Victoria Park are popular with returning students and are generally priced well below comparable cities in southern England.

**Food** — Manchester has a hugely diverse food scene. Rusholme's "Curry Mile" is one of the best-known streets for South Asian cuisine in the UK and is very affordable. Supermarkets across the city (Aldi, Lidl, Tesco, Asda) offer competitive grocery prices. Campus food outlets at both universities have subsidised options for students.

**Transport** — bus and tram costs add up over a term, but are manageable. Many students in Fallowfield walk or cycle to university, which significantly reduces spending.

**Culture and entertainment** — Manchester is one of the UK's leading cities for music, sport, nightlife, and culture. The Whitworth Gallery, Manchester Art Gallery, and Museum of Science and Industry are all free. Football is central to the city's identity — Manchester City and Manchester United both have stadium tours. Student discounts are widely accepted.

**Council Tax** — full-time students are exempt from Council Tax. Your university can provide a certificate confirming your status.

> Verify current details: rental prices, supermarket costs, and transport fares change with economic conditions. Check current listings and your university's student union cost-of-living guidance for an accurate picture.
