# Mandatories: What You MUST Do After Arriving in Australia

These are not optional. They are legal or institutional requirements tied to your Australian Student visa (subclass 500). Missing them can put your visa, your enrolment, or your healthcare access at risk. Work through them in roughly this order.

---

## 1. Enrol and Confirm Your Course (CoE)

**What it is:**
Completing formal enrolment with your university and confirming the studies on your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) — the document your education provider issued that your visa is based on.

**Why it matters:**
Your Student visa (subclass 500) is granted on the condition that you are enrolled in and studying the registered course on your CoE. Your provider reports your enrolment status to the government. If you do not enrol, or you stop studying, your visa can be cancelled. This is the Australian equivalent of the enrolment check-ins other countries require.

**What you do:**
Complete online and in-person enrolment as your university instructs, attend international student orientation, and make sure your CoE details match what you are actually studying.

*Verify the exact steps and deadlines with your university's international student office, and your visa conditions on the official source: immi.homeaffairs.gov.au.*

---

## 2. Keep Your OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover) Active

**What it is:**
Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is health insurance you are required to hold for the entire duration of your Student visa. Most students arrange it before the visa is granted, but you must make sure it is **active, paid, and covers your whole stay**.

**Why it matters:**
Maintaining adequate health insurance is a **condition of the subclass 500 visa**. Australia's healthcare is expensive without cover, and letting OSHC lapse is a visa breach. International students are generally not covered by Medicare (the public system) unless a specific reciprocal agreement applies.

**What you do:**
Confirm your OSHC policy is active and runs to the end of your visa, know your provider (e.g. Bupa, Medibank, Allianz Care, ahm, nib), and learn how to claim and find a doctor (GP). If your course is extended, extend your OSHC too.

*Verify the OSHC requirement and approved providers on the official source: health.gov.au and your visa conditions on immi.homeaffairs.gov.au.*

---

## 3. Get a Tax File Number (TFN) — If You Will Work

**What it is:**
A Tax File Number (TFN) is your personal reference number in the Australian tax system. The Student visa generally allows limited work (work-hour limits apply and can change — check your current conditions).

**Why it matters:**
If you take any paid work, you need a TFN so you are not taxed at the highest "no-TFN" rate. You can start work before it arrives, but you must apply. It is also used to set up superannuation (compulsory retirement contributions your employer pays).

**What you do:**
Apply for a TFN online through the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) after you arrive in Australia. It is free — never pay a third party for one.

*Verify the current process and eligibility on the official source: ato.gov.au — search "Tax file number." Confirm your visa's work-hour limits before taking a job.*

---

## 4. Open an Australian Bank Account

**What it is:**
An Australian transaction account so you can receive wages, pay rent, and avoid foreign-card fees.

**Why it matters:**
Australian rent, wages, and bills mostly move by bank transfer (and increasingly PayID/Osko instant transfers), which need a local account. Relying on a foreign card means fees on every purchase and difficulty paying landlords or employers.

**What you do:**
Major banks (Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ, NAB) let you open a student/everyday account, sometimes online before you arrive and then verify in branch with your passport and visa. Bring your passport, visa grant, proof of your Australian address, and your TFN once you have it.

*Verify current documentation directly with each bank before you go in. The government's Moneysmart service (moneysmart.gov.au) has plain-English guidance on bank accounts.*

---

## 5. Notify Your Provider of Your Australian Address (and Keep It Current)

**What it is:**
Giving your education provider your residential address in Australia and updating it whenever you move.

**Why it matters:**
Student visa holders are required to keep their education provider informed of their current residential address (and to update it within a set number of days of moving — commonly 7). Your provider needs to be able to contact you, and this is part of staying compliant.

**What you do:**
Provide your address through your university's student system as soon as you have one, and update it promptly each time you move. Also keep your contact details current.

*Verify the exact reporting timeframe and method with your university's international student office and your visa conditions on immi.homeaffairs.gov.au.*

---

## 6. Understand and Comply With Your Visa Conditions

**What it is:**
The specific conditions attached to your subclass 500 visa — most importantly maintaining enrolment, satisfactory course progress and attendance, keeping OSHC, and staying within work-hour limits.

**Why it matters:**
Breaching a visa condition (dropping below full enrolment, exceeding work hours, letting health cover lapse) can lead to visa cancellation. These conditions are listed on your visa grant notice and are your responsibility to know.

**What you do:**
Read your visa grant notice in full, note every condition code, and check your understanding with your international student office. When in doubt, ask before you act (e.g. before reducing your study load or taking on extra work).

*Verify your exact conditions using your visa grant notice and the official source: immi.homeaffairs.gov.au — Visa Entitlement Verification Online (VEVO) shows your current conditions.*
