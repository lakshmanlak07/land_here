# Mandatories: What You MUST Do After Arriving in Ireland

These are not optional. They are legal or institutional requirements for international students in Ireland (typically those on a Stamp 2 student permission). Missing them can put your immigration status, your bank account, or your ability to work at risk. Work through them in roughly this order.

---

## 1. Register Your Course Enrolment with Your University

**What it is:**
Completing formal registration/enrolment with your higher education institution, which confirms you as a full-time student.

**Why it matters:**
Your student permission to stay in Ireland is conditional on being enrolled in an eligible full-time course (generally on the Interim List of Eligible Programmes, ILEP). Your institution confirms your enrolment, which you'll need to prove when you register with immigration. If you don't enrol, your permission is at risk.

**What you do:**
Complete online and in-person registration as your university instructs, attend international student orientation, and obtain proof of enrolment (an enrolment letter) — you'll need it for immigration and to open a bank account.

*Verify the exact steps and required documents with your university's international student office. Eligible-course rules are on the official source: irishimmigration.ie.*

---

## 2. Register with Immigration and Get Your IRP Card

**What it is:**
If you're a non-EU/EEA student staying longer than 90 days, you must register your permission to remain and receive an **Irish Residence Permit (IRP)** card. In Dublin this is done at the Burgh Quay Registration Office; outside Dublin, at your local immigration registration office (often a Garda station).

**Why it matters:**
The IRP card is your official proof that you're legally allowed to live and study in Ireland. You need it to prove your status, re-enter the country after travel, and complete other steps. There are deadlines tied to your entry permission, and appointment slots can be limited — book as early as possible.

**What you do:**
Book an appointment (in Dublin via the online immigration appointments system). Bring your passport, your enrolment letter, proof of private medical insurance (see #5), proof of finances, and the registration fee. You'll be issued the IRP card after registering.

*Verify the current process, fees, documents, and booking system on the official source: irishimmigration.ie. Requirements and locations differ inside vs. outside Dublin.*

---

## 3. Get a PPS Number — If You Will Work or Need Public Services

**What it is:**
A **Personal Public Service (PPS) Number** is your personal reference number for Ireland's tax and public-services systems. Stamp 2 student permission generally allows limited part-time work (hour limits apply and can change — check current rules).

**Why it matters:**
If you take a job, you need a PPS number so you're taxed correctly (without it you can be emergency-taxed at a high rate). It's also used for some other services. You apply once you have an address and, usually, a reason such as employment.

**What you do:**
Apply for a PPS number through the official MyWelfare / Department of Social Protection process. You'll typically need your passport, proof of your Irish address, and evidence of your reason for needing it (e.g. a job offer).

*Verify the current application process and eligibility on the official source: gov.ie / MyWelfare. Confirm your permission's work-hour limits before taking a job.*

---

## 4. Open an Irish Bank Account

**What it is:**
An Irish current account so you can receive wages, pay rent, and avoid foreign-card fees.

**Why it matters:**
Irish rent, wages, and bills mostly move by bank transfer (SEPA), which needs an Irish IBAN. Relying on a foreign card means fees on every purchase and difficulty paying landlords or employers.

**What you do:**
Open a student/current account at a main bank (AIB, Bank of Ireland, or Permanent TSB) or an app-based bank (e.g. Revolut, N26 — widely used in Ireland). Traditional banks usually ask for your passport, your IRP card or immigration registration, proof of your Irish address, and a student enrolment letter. App-based banks can sometimes be opened faster as a stopgap.

*Verify current documentation directly with each bank before you go in. The Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (ccpc.ie) has plain-English guidance on Irish bank accounts.*

---

## 5. Hold Valid Private Medical Insurance

**What it is:**
Non-EU/EEA students are generally required to have **private medical insurance** for their stay, and you'll need to show proof of it when you register with immigration.

**Why it matters:**
It's an immigration registration requirement, and Irish healthcare is expensive without cover. International students typically don't have automatic access to free public healthcare, so insurance is your protection and a condition of registering.

**What you do:**
Arrange a policy that meets the immigration requirement (many universities offer or recommend approved student plans), and have the proof document ready for your IRP registration. Make sure it covers your whole period of stay.

*Verify the exact insurance requirement and accepted cover on the official source: irishimmigration.ie, and check whether your university offers an approved plan.*
