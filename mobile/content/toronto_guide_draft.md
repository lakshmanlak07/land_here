## Banking

Toronto has a dense concentration of bank branches and is home to all five of Canada's major banks: RBC (Royal Bank of Canada), TD Bank, Scotiabank, BMO (Bank of Montreal), and CIBC. All five offer student-specific chequing accounts that waive monthly fees while you are enrolled full-time, making them the most cost-effective option during your studies.

To open an account you will typically need your passport, your study permit (or at minimum, proof of admission), and a Canadian address. Some banks will accept a letter from your university as proof of address while you are in temporary accommodation. Branches near major campuses — including those around U of T (University of Toronto) and Ryerson (Toronto Metropolitan University) — are experienced with international student applications.

If you want an account before you arrive or in the first few days, some Canadian banks offer pre-arrival banking programmes that let you open an account online before landing. Check your chosen bank's international student page to see if this is available.

Fintech apps like Wise, Koho, and Neo Financial also offer Canadian accounts with no monthly fees and can be a useful supplement, particularly for sending money internationally.

> Verify current details: student account features, monthly fee waiver conditions, and pre-arrival banking availability change. Check each bank's website or visit your university's international student centre for current options.

## Healthcare

International students studying in Ontario have several healthcare coverage options depending on your university and your status as a visa holder.

Many Ontario universities automatically enrol their domestic students in the Ontario Health Insurance Plan (OHIP), but international students are NOT eligible for OHIP for the first three months of residence in Ontario. During this waiting period you must have private health insurance.

Most universities automatically enrol international students in a university-sponsored health and dental plan that covers this waiting period and beyond. Review what your university's plan covers — it often includes prescription drugs, dental, and vision care in addition to basic medical.

After three months of residence, if you hold a study permit of one year or more, you may become eligible to apply for OHIP. Check the province's current eligibility rules, as they have changed in recent years.

Toronto has world-class hospitals including Toronto General Hospital, Mount Sinai Hospital, and SickKids (for paediatric care). Your university's student health centre is usually the fastest route for routine GP-style appointments.

> Verify current details: OHIP eligibility rules for international students have changed before and may change again. Check the Ontario Ministry of Health website and your university's international student office for the current waiting period and eligibility criteria.

## SIM / Phone

Canada's major national carriers are Rogers, Bell, and Telus. All three operate in Toronto with strong coverage. Flanker brands — Fido (Rogers), Virgin Plus (Bell), and Koodo (Telus) — typically offer the same network quality at lower prices and are popular with students.

Canadian mobile plans have historically been more expensive than those in many other countries, but competition has increased and prices have come down over recent years, particularly for prepaid and student-targeted plans.

Prepaid SIMs from major carriers and their flanker brands can be purchased at carrier stores, Best Buy, Walmart, and convenience stores across Toronto without a credit check or SIN (Social Insurance Number). Bring your unlocked phone and passport.

For incoming students, some universities partner with carriers to offer discounted plans. Check your university's international student welcome package for details.

Wi-Fi is widely available across Toronto campuses, university residences, and many cafes and libraries, which can reduce your mobile data needs.

> Verify current details: plan pricing in Canada is competitive and changes frequently. Compare current offers on the carriers' websites or use a comparison site before purchasing, as new student deals are often available in September at the start of the academic year.

## Transport

Toronto's public transit is operated by the TTC (Toronto Transit Commission), which runs the subway, streetcars, and buses across the city.

**Presto card** is the standard reusable fare card used across TTC and regional transit. You can load money onto it at Presto machines at most subway stations, online, or at certain retailers. Using a Presto card gives you a discount versus paying the single cash fare.

**The subway** has 4 lines and connects most major university campuses to downtown and key transfer points. U of T's main campus is within walking distance of Museum and Queen's Park stations on Line 1.

**Streetcars** operate on key downtown routes along King Street, Queen Street, and Spadina Avenue. They accept Presto and contactless payment.

**Regional transit** — if you live in Mississauga, Brampton, or other GTA (Greater Toronto Area) suburbs, the regional GO Transit network (trains and buses) connects to Union Station in downtown Toronto. Presto works on GO Transit too.

**Bixi bike-share** operates in Toronto (branded as Bike Share Toronto) with hundreds of docking stations across the city. Membership plans are available and can be economical for short daily trips.

**Walking** — much of the downtown core and university district is very walkable, and many students find they primarily rely on a combination of walking and the TTC.

> Verify current details: TTC fares, Presto card loading procedures, and regional transit options change. Check the TTC website (ttc.ca) and Presto website (prestocard.ca) for current fare information.

## Cost of Living

Toronto is one of Canada's most expensive cities, with housing being the primary pressure on a student budget.

**Accommodation** — on-campus residence is the simplest option for first-year students and typically includes utilities and internet. Off-campus shared apartments or houses are more affordable per person but require more planning. Affordable student-friendly neighbourhoods include Scarborough, North York, and parts of the east end of the city, which are accessible by TTC.

**Food** — grocery shopping at chains like No Frills, Food Basics, or FreshCo is significantly cheaper than at upscale supermarkets. Cooking at home is the most cost-effective approach. Toronto has a very diverse food scene with affordable options in Kensington Market, Chinatown, Little India, and many other neighbourhoods.

**Transport** — TTC monthly passes can reduce per-trip costs if you commute regularly. Post-secondary student Presto discounts may be available; check your university's arrangements.

**Tax** — note that prices displayed in Canadian stores do not include HST (Harmonised Sales Tax, currently 13% in Ontario). This applies to most purchases and affects your effective weekly grocery and household costs.

**Entertainment** — many Toronto museums (including the Royal Ontario Museum and Art Gallery of Ontario) offer discounted student admission. The university itself often hosts free lectures, film screenings, and cultural events.

> Verify current details: rental prices, grocery costs, and tax rates change. Use current rental listings, your university's cost-of-living guide, and talk to current students before finalising your budget.
