## Banking

Sydney's banking landscape is dominated by the four major Australian banks — ANZ, Commonwealth Bank (CBA), NAB (National Australia Bank), and Westpac. All four have branches and ATMs across Sydney and near major university campuses including USYD (University of Sydney), UNSW (University of New South Wales), UTS (University of Technology Sydney), and Macquarie University.

Most of these banks offer student accounts with no monthly fees while you are enrolled full-time. To open an account, you typically need your passport and your student visa (or at minimum your Confirmation of Enrolment). Many Australian banks allow you to open an account online before you arrive and then verify your identity at a branch when you land — this can save time in the first hectic week.

Australian banks use the BSB (Bank State Branch) number and account number system, rather than IBAN. You will need your BSB and account number to receive bank transfers, including scholarship payments and salary from part-time work.

Fintech options including Wise and Up Bank are popular with students for their low fees and transparent international transfer rates.

> Verify current details: student account conditions, pre-arrival banking availability, and fee structures change. Check each bank's website or visit your university's international student support office before opening an account.

## Healthcare

International students in Australia are required to hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the duration of their student visa. OSHC is a type of private health insurance specifically designed for international students. Your university may have an arrangement with a specific OSHC provider, or you can choose from several approved providers.

OSHC covers you for visits to a GP (general practitioner), some specialist care, hospital treatment, emergency ambulance, and limited pharmaceutical benefits. OSHC does NOT cover dental or optical care by default, although add-on cover can sometimes be arranged.

You will typically need to purchase OSHC before your student visa is granted. Keep your OSHC membership card with you and bring it to any medical appointment.

Sydney has many GP clinics, including bulk-billing clinics where the consultation fee is covered entirely by OSHC with no gap payment required. Your university health or wellbeing centre can usually recommend nearby bulk-billing practices.

For emergencies, public hospital emergency departments are accessible to international students, though costs may be partially charged back through your OSHC.

> Verify current details: OSHC coverage requirements are set by the Australian government and the conditions can change. Check the Department of Home Affairs' international student pages and your specific OSHC provider's policy documents for exactly what is and is not covered.

## SIM / Phone

Getting an Australian SIM card is easy and inexpensive. You do not need proof of address or a credit check for a prepaid SIM. Major carriers include Telstra (widest coverage nationally), Optus, and Vodafone. Cheaper plans are available from sub-brands: Boost Mobile (Telstra network), Amaysim, and others.

For living in Sydney — a major metropolitan area — all three main networks provide good coverage on campuses and in the suburbs. If you plan to travel to regional areas of Australia, Telstra typically offers significantly better rural coverage.

SIM cards can be purchased at carrier stores, supermarkets (Woolworths, Coles, Big W), convenience stores, and at Sydney Airport on arrival. Bring your unlocked phone. Monthly plans start at low cost points and include data, unlimited domestic calls and texts, and sometimes international calls.

If you intend to work part-time, having a local number is essentially required for employers and job applications.

> Verify current details: carrier pricing and plan inclusions change frequently. Compare current prepaid and monthly plans on each carrier's website, or use a comparison service before committing.

## Transport

Sydney's public transport is operated by Transport for Sydney (TfNSW) and includes trains, buses, light rail, and ferries.

**Opal card** is the standard contactless smart card for all public transport in Sydney. You tap on and tap off for each journey. Opal cards can be purchased at convenience stores, newsagents, and major train stations across Sydney. You can also pay with a contactless bank card or compatible phone on most services.

**Daily and weekly fare caps** mean that once you have spent a certain amount in a day or week, further journeys are free or discounted. The weekly cap includes a half-price Sunday travel benefit.

**Trains** connect the CBD (Central Business District) to the suburbs and campuses efficiently. UNSW and Macquarie University have dedicated stops. USYD is close to Redfern and Central stations.

**Buses** are the most widespread mode of transport in Sydney and serve many areas trains do not reach. Routes operate frequently in the inner suburbs.

**Light rail** runs through the CBD and extends to areas including Kingsford (near UNSW) and Randwick.

**Ferries** operate on Sydney Harbour and are a scenic option for students living in the northern suburbs or Manly.

**Cycling** infrastructure in Sydney has expanded, with cycleways across many inner suburbs. Lime and Neuron e-scooters also operate in some areas.

> Verify current details: Opal card prices, fare caps, and network changes are updated regularly. Check transportnsw.info for current fares, maps, and any planned service changes.

## Cost of Living

Sydney is consistently ranked among the world's most expensive cities to live in, with housing being the greatest financial challenge for international students.

**Accommodation** — on-campus university halls are the most convenient option for new arrivals but are limited in number. Many universities guarantee first-year students a place if they apply early enough. Off-campus shared accommodation in suburbs like Kingsford, Ultimo, Chippendale, Zetland, and Newtown is popular with students and can be significantly more affordable per person.

**Food** — supermarkets such as Aldi, Woolworths, and Coles are your main options for grocery shopping. Cooking at home is far cheaper than eating out. Sydney has a diverse dining scene with affordable options in its many cultural precincts (Haymarket's Chinatown, Cabramatta, Lakemba, and others), though central city cafes and restaurants can be expensive.

**Transport** — if your accommodation is within walking or cycling distance of campus, you can reduce transport costs considerably. The weekly Opal cap limits the maximum you can spend on transport in any given week.

**Part-time work** — international students on a student visa are permitted to work a limited number of hours per week during semester. Sydney has a large hospitality, retail, and campus employment market. Your university's career services office can help with job searching.

**Taxes and costs to be aware of** — prices displayed in Australian stores include GST (Goods and Services Tax, currently 10%). International students are subject to Australian tax law on earnings from Australian employment.

> Verify current details: rental prices, grocery costs, and working-hour allowances for student visas change. Use current rental listings, your university's cost-of-living estimates, and check the Department of Home Affairs' website for current student visa work conditions.
