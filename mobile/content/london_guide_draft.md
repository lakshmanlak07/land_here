## Banking

London is home to every major UK bank as well as a large concentration of challenger banks and fintech apps that are particularly well-suited to international students.

The main high-street banks — Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, and Santander — all have student accounts with no monthly fee and typically include a student railcard or cashback offer. Opening one usually requires proof of your university enrolment, your passport, and a UK address. Many university campuses have branch offices or dedicated student banking desks.

If you arrive before your high-street account is ready, fintech apps like Monzo, Revolut, and Wise can issue you a UK account number and sort code quickly, often within the same day and without a UK credit history. These are widely accepted for receiving money, paying rent by bank transfer, and making card payments.

Most London universities give you an IBAN-compatible bank account number through Convera or a similar service for receiving fee payments and scholarships from abroad.

> Verify current details: student account features, eligibility requirements, and bonus offers change each academic year. Check each bank's website or visit your university's student services office for the most up-to-date options.

## Healthcare

The National Health Service (NHS) provides healthcare to international students in the United Kingdom. As a student on a visa of more than six months, you will typically pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) as part of your visa application, which gives you access to NHS services during your studies.

Registering with a GP (General Practitioner — your local doctor) is your first step. Most universities have an on-campus health centre or a list of recommended nearby GP practices where students can register easily. NHS GP appointments are free at the point of use once you are registered.

For emergency care, NHS Accident and Emergency (A&E) departments are available 24/7 at major hospitals across London. For non-emergency medical advice at any time, call NHS 111 (free).

London has world-class hospitals including University College Hospital, King's College Hospital, and St Thomas' Hospital. Dental care is partially covered by the NHS but waiting lists can be long; many students use private dental practices for routine check-ups.

> Verify current details: NHS registration procedures, the IHS amount, and what it covers are set by the UK government and can change. Check NHS.uk and the UK government's visa guidance for the most current information before you travel.

## SIM / Phone

Getting a UK SIM card is straightforward and inexpensive. You do not need proof of address or a credit check for a prepaid (pay-as-you-go) SIM, and most carriers allow you to convert to a monthly rolling SIM-only plan later.

Major carriers operating in London include EE (generally rated best for coverage), Vodafone, O2, and Three. All have stores in central London and at major transport hubs like King's Cross, Waterloo, and Heathrow. Supermarkets such as Sainsbury's, Tesco, and Asda also sell SIM cards at low cost.

Network coverage in central London and on university campuses is generally excellent. Underground (Tube) coverage has been expanded progressively but is not complete across all lines and stations.

For students who do a lot of international calling, virtual SIM providers or calling apps (like WhatsApp or Google Voice) are often more economical than carrier international plans.

> Verify current details: network coverage, plan pricing, and which supermarkets stock which SIMs change regularly. Check the carrier's website or visit a store in person to compare current monthly deals before committing.

## Transport

London has one of the most comprehensive public transport networks in the world, operated by Transport for London (TfL).

**The Oyster card or contactless payment** is the standard way to pay for Tube, bus, Overground, Elizabeth line, and most rail travel within Greater London. There is no need to buy a paper ticket for most journeys. An Oyster card requires a small refundable deposit; contactless bank card or phone payments work identically.

**Daily and weekly caps** mean that once you have spent a certain amount in a day or week, all further travel within the same zones is free. This makes daily commuting cost-effective.

**Zones** — London's transport network is divided into Zones 1–9. Most university campuses and student accommodation are in Zones 1–4. Fares increase with distance.

**Buses** are frequent, run 24/7 on many routes, and are the cheapest way to get around within a zone. Cash is not accepted on London buses; you must use Oyster or contactless.

**Cycling** — Santander Cycles (commonly called Boris Bikes) are available across central London with docking stations near most major universities. A short hire gives you unlimited free journeys under a certain duration.

**18+ Student Oyster card** — full-time students in London can apply for a discounted Oyster card that gives a percentage off Travelcard and Bus & Tram Pass prices. This requires a valid TfL application through your university.

> Verify current details: fares, zones, capping amounts, and student discount eligibility change. Check tfl.gov.uk for current fare tables and the student Oyster application process.

## Cost of Living

London is one of the most expensive cities in the world to live in, particularly for housing. Budgeting carefully is essential.

**Accommodation** is the largest cost. On-campus university halls are convenient for first-year students and typically include utility bills, internet, and sometimes a meal plan. Off-campus shared house or flat rentals in affordable student-friendly areas — such as Stratford, Walthamstow, Lewisham, or parts of south-east London — are significantly cheaper per month than central London or Zones 1–2 locations.

**Food** costs depend heavily on your habits. Cooking at home using supermarkets like Aldi, Lidl, or the basics ranges at Tesco, Sainsbury's, and Asda can be quite economical. Central London restaurants and cafes are expensive. Many universities have subsidised cafeterias.

**Transport** is manageable with an Oyster card and the daily cap system, but costs add up. If your accommodation is within walking or cycling distance of your university, you can save substantially.

**Entertainment** — London's museums and galleries (British Museum, National Gallery, Tate Modern, Natural History Museum, and many more) are free to enter. Student discounts are widely available for theatre, cinema, and attractions with a valid student ID (NUS card or ISIC).

**Council Tax** — full-time students are usually exempt from paying Council Tax. Your university can provide a certificate to confirm your student status if your landlord or local council requires it.

> Verify current details: rental prices, council tax exemption rules, and costs shift with economic conditions. Use current rental listings, your university's student services cost-of-living estimates, and talk to current students before finalising your budget.
