# Mandatories: What You MUST Do After Arriving in Germany

These are not optional. They are legal or institutional requirements for international students in Germany. Several of them depend on each other (you often need one to do the next), so order matters. Work through them in roughly this sequence.

---

## 1. Register Your Address (Anmeldung)

**What it is:**
The **Anmeldung** is registering your residential address with the local registration office (Bürgeramt / Einwohnermeldeamt). After registering you receive a confirmation document, the **Meldebescheinigung**.

**Why it matters:**
This is the keystone of German bureaucracy. You typically **cannot open a bank account, get a residence permit, or sign many contracts without your Anmeldung confirmation**. It is also a legal requirement — you must register within a set number of days of moving in (commonly within ~14 days, varies by city), and booking the appointment (Termin) can take weeks, so act immediately.

**What you do:**
Book a Termin at your local Bürgeramt as soon as you have an address. Bring your passport, your rental contract, and a **Wohnungsgeberbestätigung** (a confirmation from your landlord that you live there). Get several copies of the resulting Meldebescheinigung — you'll need it repeatedly.

*Verify the deadline, required documents, and booking process on your specific city's official website (search "[your city] Anmeldung Bürgeramt"). Rules and timeframes vary by municipality.*

---

## 2. Enrol at Your University (Immatrikulation)

**What it is:**
Formal enrolment (Immatrikulation/Einschreibung) at your university, which makes you an official student and produces your student ID and enrolment certificate.

**Why it matters:**
Your residence permit for study is conditional on being enrolled. Enrolment also unlocks your Semesterticket, library access, and student status for discounts. You usually must pay the semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag) to complete it.

**What you do:**
Complete enrolment as your university instructs — this typically requires proof of health insurance (see #3), your passport/visa, your university entrance qualification, and payment of the semester fee. Attend any international student orientation.

*Verify the exact documents and deadlines with your university's international office (Akademisches Auslandsamt / International Office). Requirements vary by institution.*

---

## 3. Get German Health Insurance (Krankenversicherung)

**What it is:**
Health insurance is **mandatory** in Germany and is a prerequisite for enrolment. Students under a certain age are usually eligible for **public (statutory) insurance** (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung — e.g. TK, AOK, Barmer); some students (e.g. certain ages or funding situations) take **private** cover instead.

**Why it matters:**
You **cannot enrol (Immatrikulation) without proof of valid health insurance**, and being insured is a legal requirement. Public insurers also issue an electronic health card (Gesundheitskarte) you use at the doctor.

**What you do:**
Choose a public insurer (rates for students are broadly similar across providers), apply, and get the confirmation your university needs for enrolment. If you arrive on travel insurance, switch to compliant German cover quickly. Confirm whether you qualify for public or must take private cover.

*Verify your eligibility and the accepted insurers with your university's international office and the official sources for German statutory health insurance. Don't rely on travel insurance for enrolment.*

---

## 4. Open a German Bank Account (Girokonto)

**What it is:**
A current account (Girokonto) for receiving money, paying rent (usually by SEPA transfer or Lastschrift/direct debit), and everyday spending.

**Why it matters:**
German rent, the broadcasting fee, insurance, and many bills are paid by SEPA transfer or direct debit, which need a German IBAN. If you are on a visa that required a **blocked account (Sperrkonto)** to prove funds, you'll also draw your monthly living allowance from it — set up access to it early.

**What you do:**
Open a Girokonto at a traditional bank (e.g. Sparkasse, Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank) or an app-based bank (e.g. N26, with caveats). You'll usually need your passport, your Anmeldung confirmation, your enrolment certificate, and sometimes your residence permit. Then arrange access to your Sperrkonto if you have one.

*Verify current documentation directly with each bank. Confirm blocked-account (Sperrkonto) rules on the official source: the German Federal Foreign Office (auswaertiges-amt.de) and your visa conditions.*

---

## 5. Apply for Your Residence Permit (Aufenthaltstitel)

**What it is:**
If you are from a country whose nationals need a residence permit, you must apply for one at the local **Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde)** after arriving, even if you entered on a national visa. (Some nationalities can enter visa-free and apply for the permit in Germany — check your case.)

**Why it matters:**
The permit is your legal authorisation to stay and study beyond your entry visa. Appointments can take a long time to get, and your entry visa has an expiry — apply well before it runs out.

**What you do:**
Book a Termin at your city's Ausländerbehörde as early as possible. You'll typically need your passport, Anmeldung confirmation, enrolment certificate, proof of health insurance, proof of finances (often the Sperrkonto), biometric photos, and the fee. Bring everything; missing documents mean another long wait.

*Verify the exact documents, fees, and booking process on your city's Ausländerbehörde website and the official source: the German Federal Foreign Office (auswaertiges-amt.de). Requirements vary by city and nationality.*

---

## 6. Register for the Broadcasting Fee (Rundfunkbeitrag)

**What it is:**
The **Rundfunkbeitrag** is a mandatory public broadcasting fee charged **per household**, regardless of whether you own a TV or radio.

**Why it matters:**
It is a legal obligation, not optional, and it is billed per dwelling. If you live alone you'll be liable; in a shared flat (WG) usually only **one** registration per household is needed, so coordinate with flatmates to avoid paying twice. Ignoring the letters that arrive after your Anmeldung can lead to enforced collection.

**What you do:**
After your Anmeldung you'll likely receive a letter. Register your household (or confirm a flatmate already pays for it) via the official Rundfunkbeitrag service. Certain recipients of state support may be exempt or reduced — check if that applies.

*Verify the current fee, who pays, and exemptions on the official source: rundfunkbeitrag.de.*
