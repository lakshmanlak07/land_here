## Banking

Melbourne has a strong presence of all four major Australian banks — ANZ, Commonwealth Bank (CBA), NAB, and Westpac — with branches and ATMs across the CBD (Central Business District), near major university campuses, and throughout the suburbs.

All four offer student chequing accounts with no monthly fees while you are enrolled full-time. Opening one typically requires your passport and your student visa (or your Confirmation of Enrolment). Many banks — including CBA and ANZ — offer pre-arrival account opening for international students arriving at Australian universities, letting you set up an account before you land. Check your chosen bank's international student section.

The University of Melbourne campus and RMIT University (both in Parkville and the CBD respectively) are surrounded by bank branches. Monash University's Clayton campus also has nearby banking options.

Fintech services like Wise and Up Bank offer Australian account numbers with a BSB and account number, useful in the first days before your in-person verification at a branch. Cash machines are widely available in Melbourne's CBD, Swanston Street, and the main shopping precincts.

> Verify current details: pre-arrival banking availability, student account conditions, and fee structures change. Check each bank's website or your university's international office for current options.

## Healthcare

International students studying in Australia must hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the full duration of their student visa. OSHC is a type of private health insurance specifically designed for international students and is a visa requirement.

Your university may have an arrangement with a specific OSHC provider, or you can select from a list of government-approved providers. OSHC covers GP consultations, some specialist visits, hospital treatment, emergency ambulance, and a portion of prescription costs. Most standard OSHC plans do not include dental or optical; these require additional cover.

Many Melbourne clinics, including those near the University of Melbourne, RMIT, and Monash, are familiar with OSHC and can bulk-bill under it (meaning you pay no out-of-pocket fee for the consultation). Your university's student health service is usually the fastest and most convenient route for routine care and referrals.

For emergencies, Melbourne's Royal Melbourne Hospital, Alfred Hospital, and St Vincent's Hospital all have 24/7 emergency departments.

> Verify current details: OSHC requirements are set by the Australian government. The list of approved providers, what is covered, and the cost can change. Check the Department of Home Affairs website and your specific OSHC policy document for exact coverage.

## SIM / Phone

Melbourne has excellent mobile coverage from Australia's three major carriers: Telstra, Optus, and Vodafone. Telstra generally offers the widest coverage nationally, which matters more if you plan to travel outside major cities.

Prepaid SIMs are readily available at carrier stores, supermarkets (Woolworths, Coles, Big W), chemists (Chemist Warehouse, Priceline), and at Melbourne Airport on arrival. No credit check or proof of address is needed — bring your unlocked phone and identity document for in-store activation.

For campus areas, all major carriers have strong coverage across the University of Melbourne (Parkville), RMIT (CBD), and Monash (Clayton). Monthly SIM-only plans without a contract are available from all carriers and offer the most flexibility. Plans typically include unlimited Australian calls and texts, a data allowance, and sometimes international calling credits.

Melbourne's universities also offer free eduroam Wi-Fi across campus buildings, available to students from partner institutions worldwide.

> Verify current details: plan pricing, coverage maps, and in-store availability change frequently. Compare plans on each carrier's website before purchasing.

## Transport

Melbourne's public transport network is operated by Public Transport Victoria (PTV) and includes trams, trains, and buses.

**The Myki card** is the standard reusable smartcard for all Melbourne public transport. You tap on and tap off for trains and trams; buses require a tap on (and tap off where possible). Myki cards can be purchased at train stations, PTV hubs, and many 7-Eleven stores. Contactless bank card payment is also accepted on most Melbourne public transport services.

**Trams** — Melbourne has the largest urban tram network in the world. Free tram travel is available within the Melbourne CBD Free Tram Zone, which covers Swanston Street, Elizabeth Street, Flinders Street, and other central city streets. This is extremely useful for students at RMIT and other CBD-based institutions.

**Trains** — the metropolitan rail network connects Melbourne's suburbs and campuses to Flinders Street Station and the City Loop. Monash University's Clayton campus is served by the Huntingdale or Oakleigh station on the Pakenham/Cranbourne lines. The University of Melbourne's Parkville campus is a short tram or walk from the CBD.

**Cycling** — Melbourne has a growing network of cycling lanes. The Yarra River trail and the city's inner suburbs are well served by bike paths. Melbourne Bike Share (currently operated under various schemes) offers short-term hiring near major attractions.

**Student concession Myki** — full-time students may be eligible for a concession Myki, which provides a significant discount on fares. Eligibility and the application process are managed by PTV and require a valid student ID. Check ptv.vic.gov.au for the current concession card process for international students.

> Verify current details: Myki fares, the CBD Free Tram Zone boundaries, and student concession eligibility are subject to change. Check ptv.vic.gov.au for current information.

## Cost of Living

Melbourne is one of Australia's most expensive cities, though generally somewhat less expensive than Sydney. Housing is the largest budget item for most international students.

**Accommodation** — the University of Melbourne and Monash University both offer residential colleges and student accommodation. Apply as early as possible, as places for international students are competitive. Off-campus shared accommodation in inner suburbs close to the University of Melbourne (such as Carlton, Fitzroy, Parkville, and Brunswick) is popular with students but commands relatively high rents. Suburbs further from the CBD along train lines — Clayton (near Monash), Caulfield, or Box Hill — can offer more affordable shared rentals.

**Food** — Melbourne has one of Australia's most celebrated and diverse food scenes. Affordable eating options are found across the city: Lygon Street for Italian, Victoria Street (Richmond) for Vietnamese, and Footscray for a range of African, Vietnamese, and South Asian cuisine. Supermarkets including Aldi, Woolworths, and Coles offer competitive grocery prices. Cooking at home is substantially cheaper than dining out regularly. The university areas have subsidised student cafeterias (Baillieu Library café at UoM, various campus eateries at Monash) with reasonable prices.

**Transport** — if your student concession Myki is approved, public transport costs are significantly reduced. If you live close enough to cycle, transport costs can be minimal.

**Culture and entertainment** — Melbourne is one of the most culturally active cities in the Southern Hemisphere. The National Gallery of Victoria (NGV) has free permanent collection access. The Melbourne Museum and State Library of Victoria are both free. Melbourne's live music scene is legendary, and many venues have free or low-cost entry, particularly on weeknights. The Melbourne International Film Festival (MIFF) and the Melbourne Comedy Festival offer student-priced tickets.

**Taxes** — prices in Australian stores include GST (Goods and Services Tax, currently 10%). Basic grocery staples are generally GST-free; most other goods and services are taxed.

> Verify current details: rental prices, grocery costs, and student concession eligibility change regularly. Use current rental listings, your university's student services cost-of-living guide, and talk to current students for a realistic picture before finalising your budget.
