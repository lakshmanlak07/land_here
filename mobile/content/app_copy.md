Polished, warm, on-brand copy you can drop into the UI later. Nothing here is wired
into the app — these are options and ready-to-use strings. Pick what fits; tweak
freely.

move — reassuring, plain-spoken, never bureaucratic, never hype. Short sentences.

## 4. "How we rank" — methodology, friendly plain English

Polished copy for the methodology screen. Matches the current model:
two halves (Research 50%, Outcomes 50%), five factors, sources College Scorecard
and OpenAlex.

**Intro**
> ### The LandHere Score
> The LandHere Score is our own honest measure of a university, built only from open
> public data. It isn't an opinion poll, a reputation survey, or a paid placement —
> **no one can pay to move up**. Every university is measured exactly the same way,
> so what you see reflects evidence, not prestige.

**What goes into it**
> The score is split evenly between the two things that matter most: the **research**
> a university produces, and the **real-world outcomes** for the students who go
> there.

> **Research — 50%**
> How much knowledge the university creates, and how far it reaches.
> - **Output** — how much published work it produces.
> - **Impact** — how often that work is cited by others.

> **Outcomes — 50%**
> What actually happens to students after they enrol.
> - **Earnings** — what graduates typically earn, years down the line.
> - **Graduation rate** — how many students finish what they start.
> - **Affordability** — the real cost relative to the value you get.

> Five factors in total — two for research, three for outcomes — with each half
> contributing an equal 50% to the final number.

**Where the numbers come from**
> - **College Scorecard** (U.S. Department of Education) — tuition, net price,
>   graduation rates, and post-graduation earnings.
> - **OpenAlex** (open catalogue of scholarly works) — research output and citation
>   impact across publications.

**The honest caveat**
> ### Treat it as a starting point
> The LandHere Score is a model built from public data, so it only reflects the
> factors above. It can't capture everything that matters to *you* — your specific
> programme, campus culture, location, scholarships, or how a place feels. Use it to
> shortlist and compare, then always do your own research before you decide.

**One-line version (for a tooltip or the rankings sub-header)**
> An objective score from open public data — half research, half student outcomes.
> No opinions, no paid placements.

## 6. Tone do / don't

**Do**
- Be specific and reassuring: "You'll usually need X before you can do Y."
- Acknowledge the stress, then make it manageable.
- Always point to the official source for anything that can change.

**Don't**
- Don't give legal/immigration advice or imply certainty about rules.
- Don't use bureaucratic phrasing ("applicants must furnish documentation").
- Don't over-promise ("we handle everything for you").
- Don't invent fees, deadlines, or amounts — these change and vary.
