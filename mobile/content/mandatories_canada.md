# Mandatories: What You MUST Do After Arriving in Canada

These are not optional. They are legal or institutional requirements for international students holding a Canadian **Study Permit**. Breaking the conditions on your permit can put your status — and your ability to stay and work — at risk. Work through them in roughly this order.

---

## 1. Check and Comply With the Conditions on Your Study Permit

**What it is:**
When you land in Canada, a **Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)** officer issues your physical **Study Permit**. It lists conditions: which type of school you may attend, your permit expiry date, and any work limits or restrictions. You must read it before you leave the immigration hall.

**Why it matters:**
Your permit is the legal basis for everything that follows. To keep it valid you generally must remain **actively enrolled at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)**, make reasonable progress toward completing your program, and respect any work-hour limits. Falling out of these conditions can affect your status and future applications.

**What you do:**
Read the permit on the spot. Confirm your name, date of birth, expiry date, the DLI condition, and any work conditions are correct. If anything is wrong, tell the CBSA officer immediately. Keep the permit safe — you will need it constantly.

*Verify the current conditions and your obligations on the official source: canada.ca — IRCC "Study Permit: study conditions / maintain your status." Rules on enrolment and work hours change; confirm what applies to you.*

---

## 2. Confirm Enrolment and Register With Your University

**What it is:**
Completing official enrolment with your **Designated Learning Institution (DLI)** and checking in with its international student services office.

**Why it matters:**
Your Study Permit requires you to be enrolled and progressing at a DLI. Canadian institutions must report on international student enrolment to IRCC. If you do not enrol, your compliance can be questioned, which can affect your status.

**What you do:**
Complete course registration and any international student check-in your university requires, and attend mandatory orientation. Bring your passport, Study Permit, and proof of your Canadian address.

*Verify exact steps and deadlines through your specific university's international student office. This is set by your institution, not by a single government page.*

---

## 3. Apply for a Social Insurance Number (SIN) — If You Will Work

**What it is:**
A **Social Insurance Number (SIN)** is a 9-digit number required to work in Canada, file taxes, and access certain government services. Most Study Permits allow some work (on and, within limits, off campus during academic sessions — check your permit and current rules).

**Why it matters:**
You cannot be paid legally or file a tax return without a SIN. If your Study Permit authorises work, getting a SIN early means you are ready when a job comes up. If your permit does not authorise work, you may not be eligible yet.

**What you do:**
Apply at a **Service Canada Centre** in person (or online, where available) with your passport and your Study Permit showing the work-eligibility condition. The SIN itself is free — never pay a third party for it.

*Verify current eligibility and how to apply on the official source: canada.ca — Service Canada "Apply for a Social Insurance Number." Confirm your permit actually authorises work first.*

---

## 4. Register for Provincial / Territorial Health Coverage (and Bridge the Gap)

**What it is:**
Healthcare in Canada is run by each province and territory. Depending on where you study, international students may be eligible for **public provincial health coverage** (for example in some provinces) or may need to rely on a **university / private health plan** — and several provinces impose a waiting period before public coverage starts.

**Why it matters:**
Medical care without coverage is very expensive. Many universities automatically enrol international students into a mandatory health plan (often through the student union) precisely to cover this gap. You must understand which scheme covers you and from what date, so you are never uninsured.

**What you do:**
Find out your province's rules and whether you qualify for its public plan. If there is a waiting period, confirm your university's mandatory or supplementary plan covers you in the meantime. Apply for the provincial card if you are eligible — bring your Study Permit, passport, and proof of address.

*Verify on the official source: your province's or territory's health ministry website (rules differ by province), and your university's student health plan page. Do not assume coverage starts on arrival.*

---

## 5. Open a Canadian Bank Account

**What it is:**
A Canadian chequing account for receiving funds, paying rent, and avoiding foreign card fees.

**Why it matters:**
Rent, wages, and many bills run through Canadian banking. A local account avoids per-transaction foreign fees and lets employers and landlords pay or charge you normally. It is also the foundation for building a Canadian credit history.

**What you do:**
The major banks (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC) offer no-fee student chequing accounts. You will typically need your Study Permit, passport, and a Canadian address. Some banks let you open an account before arrival through newcomer programs — worth checking.

*Verify document requirements directly with the bank. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (canada.ca — "Opening a bank account") has plain-English, government guidance on your banking rights.*
